ReadyFS demo recording preferences

User preference, updated 12 October 2026. Apply by default to future recordings and edits.

  • Present command typing and input editing about 40% faster (1.4×) than the original v4 human-paced tour. This is a playback rate, not 40% less time. Preserve clear keystrokes and command editing.
  • Tighten idle waits and external test-automation gaps. Leave enough time to read new results and pages; ordinary page-reading pauses should generally be about 3–5 seconds.
  • Keep filesystem operations/results at their recorded speed. Use clearly labelled cuts for long idle periods; do not suggest the C64 transferred data faster than it did.
  • Avoid long repetitive text-entry demonstrations. A labelled jump through repeated text is appropriate; retain the complete long line and all meaningful editing actions.
  • Descriptions must be concise: short title plus one sentence, normally ten words or fewer, shown for roughly 4–5 seconds.
  • Put description cards in a separate top band above the complete C64 picture. Never place captions or descriptions over console output or the input area. Use navigation-only chapter tracks, not full-chapter descriptive subtitles.
  • Preserve varied commands, exact aliases/options, path boundaries, file-by-file previews, and meaningful outcomes. Remap chapter/subchapter and script timestamps after every edit.
  • Keep download buttons and the printable/Markdown guide synchronized with the published cut. Preserve source recordings and evidence.
  • Physical recording still uses the normal keyboard-consumption and native UCI protocol safety checks. Playback editing must not weaken device pacing, ownership checks, source preservation or restoration.

The current request is an edit of existing footage. Do not rerecord merely to apply these preferences.

Comprehension edit (12 October)

  • Budget time by meaning: introduce a concept, show its action, then let the viewer recognize its result. Do not remove pauses solely because the screen is still.
  • Give new selections and dense examples more reading time than repeated syntax or results. These are editorial defaults, not measured reading limits.
  • After demonstrating paging, omit redundant intermediate directory pages with a visible notice. Keep the first page, useful context and final outcome.
  • Condense repeated command entry while showing the complete final command. Preserve actual insertion, overwrite, Home/End and history/page transitions.
  • Shorten per-file transfer waits while retaining every named completion and batch summary. Label the transfer section as condensed.
  • Keep the established 1.4× input presentation; use selective cuts for further savings.
  • Preserve the original take, screenshots and hardware evidence, and remap all chapter/step timestamps.
